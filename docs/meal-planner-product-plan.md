# Vetify meal planner: product and implementation plan

Research checked 1 October 2026. Scope: household dogs and cats. This document proposes product behavior; it is not a feeding prescription. The companion [nutrition research](meal-planner-research.md) has the clinical detail behind the rules below.

## Product decision

Vetify currently saves pet profiles but does not create or run a meal plan ([README](../README.md)). The first real planner should help an owner answer four daily questions: **what food, how much, when, and did the pet eat it?** For a healthy adult dog or cat with suitable inputs, it may calculate a clearly labeled **starting portion estimate**. For other pets, it may schedule an amount already supplied by the owner or veterinarian, without calculating a new prescription. A seven-day view is a reusable schedule, not seven different recipes or proof of nutritional adequacy.

Success means an owner can create a plan for one of several pets, understand its source, record actual feeding, and notice when the plan needs review. Do not launch as a recipe generator, disease diet selector, allergy diagnosis tool, or weight-loss prescriber. [AAHA feeding plans](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/feeding-plans-for-healthy-appropriate-weight-cats-and-dogs/), [AAHA nutritional risk factors](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/nutritional-risk-factors/), [AAHA home-prepared diets](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/home-prepared-diets/).

The product inference is a loop: **profile and constraints -> food and amount -> schedule -> actual intake -> weight and condition -> review**. A static pet card stops before the useful part of that loop.

## Nutrition rules that shape the design

1. **Start with a suitable complete food.** Capture the adequacy statement, intended species and life stage, exact formula, and energy density. A treat or food labeled for supplemental feeding is not a complete daily base. US labels often give kcal/kg plus kcal per familiar unit, but label conventions vary by market. A barcode or brand name alone does not verify calories. [AAFCO label guide](https://aafco.org/consumers/understanding-pet-food/reading-labels/), [WSAVA food selection guide](https://wsava.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Selecting-a-pet-food-for-your-pet-updated-2021_WSAVA-Global-Nutrition-Toolkit.pdf), [FEDIAF guidelines](https://www.fediaf.org/self-regulation/nutritional-guidelines/).
2. **Energy math gives a starting point.** `RER = 70 x weightKg^0.75` kcal/day. An adult maintenance factor or measured stable intake gives an initial daily target, but individual needs vary substantially. Show the factor, inputs and date; never present the output as an exact requirement. Weight and body condition trends guide later review. [Merck nutritional requirements](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/management-and-nutrition/nutrition-small-animals/nutritional-requirements-of-small-animals), [AAHA feeding plans](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/feeding-plans-for-healthy-appropriate-weight-cats-and-dogs/).
3. **Count all edible calories.** Record base food, treats, chews, toppers, table food, supplements with calories, and food used for medication. AAHA's general guidance keeps at least 90% of calories from complete food and no more than 10% from extras. This is a warning threshold, not an instruction to reserve 10% when the pet has no treats. [AAHA feeding plans](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/feeding-plans-for-healthy-appropriate-weight-cats-and-dogs/).
4. **Condition changes eligibility.** Growing, pregnant or nursing pets, pets with disease, muscle loss, unusual weight change, or body condition outside the ideal 4-5/9 range need a more detailed assessment. The current 1-10 field is incompatible with the usual 9-point dog/cat chart. Add a new 1-9 observation and preserve old values as legacy data; never silently convert them. Owner-entered scores should say who made the observation. [AAHA risk factors](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/nutritional-risk-factors/), [WSAVA body condition charts](https://wsava.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/WSAVA_BCSCat_BCSDog_Nutrition_250612.pdf).
5. **Meal frequency is configurable.** Adult dogs commonly receive measured meals twice daily; puppies usually need more frequent meals. Cats often benefit from at least two and sometimes several smaller meals. A medical schedule can differ. Default suggestions may help, but the owner chooses count and times, or follows a veterinarian's schedule. Multi-pet access matters. [Merck feeding practices](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/management-and-nutrition/nutrition-small-animals/feeding-practices-in-small-animals), [VCA cat feeding frequency](https://vcahospitals.com/boulevard/know-your-pet/feeding-times-and-frequency-for-cats), [CatVets feeding consensus](https://catvets.com/resource/how-to-feed-how-to-feed-a-cat-consensus-statement/).
6. **Restrictions need provenance.** Store each reported food reaction separately, with suspected or veterinarian-confirmed status and the reaction. A comma-separated text field may become an array of terms, but it cannot establish diagnosis or that a product is allergen-free. For elimination diets and serious reactions, schedule only the veterinarian-selected food and all allowed extras. [Merck food allergy review](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/integumentary-system/food-allergy/cutaneous-food-allergy-in-animals).
7. **Do not formulate from human recipes.** Cats have species-specific nutrient requirements, and home recipes commonly miss essential nutrients. Any future home-prepared diet path needs veterinary nutritionist formulation and exact recipe adherence. Filter hazardous human foods out of optional treat suggestions. [Merck nutritional requirements](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/management-and-nutrition/nutrition-small-animals/nutritional-requirements-of-small-animals), [AAHA home-prepared diets](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/home-prepared-diets/), [ASPCA food hazards](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/people-foods-avoid-feeding-your-pets).

## Owner flows

### 1. First plan

1. Select a pet card and tap **Plan meals**. Show a short setup checklist, saved draft state, and a clear **Schedule existing amount** route at the start.
2. Confirm age source, current measured weight and date, recent weight change, and 9-point body condition if known. Ask yes/no/unknown about growing status, pregnancy or nursing, significant illness, prescribed diet, and recent appetite change. This is a screen for calculation eligibility, not diagnosis.
3. Choose the calculation route. **Estimate starting amount** is offered only to an apparently healthy, stable adult dog or cat with suitable data. **Schedule existing amount** accepts a measured owner or veterinarian amount for any pet, including higher-risk cases. If answers are missing, explain which input is needed; do not guess.
4. Add the exact current food. Ask for brand, product, formula, dry/wet form, species and life-stage adequacy, label kcal/kg or kcal/package and net grams, source, date checked and optional label photo. Confirm all unit conversions before continuing. Warn if the food is supplemental or its adequacy cannot be established. Keep the user on the schedule-only route if energy density is unknown.
5. Add extras and how much is actually given. Show daily calories from extras when known, and an **unknown calories** state when not. Separate preferences, reported reactions, medical restrictions and exclusions in the interface.
6. Enter meals per day, times, desired split, feeding location and whether other pets can reach the food. Suggest two measured meals for an adult dog as an editable starting schedule, never a hard rule. For cats, support more smaller meals and timed feeders.
7. Show a review screen with total daily kcal if calculated, base-food grams/day, amount per meal, extras, calorie source, weight date, assumption used, and warnings. The owner taps **Save plan**. This is the only step that creates an active plan.
8. Open **Today** with the next meal, measured amount, remaining planned meals and a **Log feeding** action. The week view repeats the chosen schedule, with editable exceptions for travel or appointments.

### 2. Daily use and change

- At each meal, record fed, partially fed, skipped or unknown, with actual grams or package amount, time, caregiver and optional appetite/vomiting/stool note. Never mark a meal eaten just because a reminder fired. A missed meal can be rescheduled; the app must not silently add its calories to the next one.
- Log treats separately or as part of a meal. Display **planned / reported actual / unknown** instead of inventing intake when food is left in a bowl, another pet has access, or no one logged the meal.
- Allow editing future times and splits without changing the daily total. A food substitution opens a new review because kcal density and adequacy may differ. When changing products, show gradual transition guidance; AAHA describes 4-7 days for many changes, subject to veterinary advice. [AAHA feeding plans](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/feeding-plans-for-healthy-appropriate-weight-cats-and-dogs/).
- Weight, body condition, appetite or health changes trigger **Review plan**. Show old and proposed amounts side by side, plus why they differ. The old plan and logs remain available. No silent recalculation or automatic therapeutic adjustment.
- A sudden refusal to eat, repeated vomiting, or a serious suspected reaction opens an appropriate veterinary help prompt instead of a suggestion to compensate at the next meal. [AAHA nutritional risk factors](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/nutritional-risk-factors/).

### 3. Higher-risk and missing-data paths

| Situation                                                                                                                                         | Vetify behavior                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Healthy, stable adult dog/cat with dated weight, condition assessment, verified complete food, calories and known extras                          | Offer a starting portion estimate and schedule.                                                                                    |
| Missing or stale weight, unknown condition, no verifiable energy density, uncertain life stage, or unquantified extras                            | Ask for data. Permit a measured existing-amount schedule, label the unknowns, and withhold a calculated portion.                   |
| Puppy/kitten, pregnancy/nursing, weight-loss or gain treatment, disease, prescribed diet, unexplained weight/appetite change, or serious reaction | Record the veterinarian's or owner's existing food and amount. Show a veterinary review prompt. Do not suggest a new dose or diet. |
| Other species, homemade/raw recipe, or food that is not a suitable complete diet                                                                  | No automatic nutrition calculation. Show only a clearly identified manual schedule if appropriate.                                 |

These are proposed Vetify product gates based on [AAHA nutritional risk factors](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/nutritional-risk-factors/) and [food adequacy guidance](https://aafco.org/consumers/understanding-pet-food/reading-labels/). A veterinarian should review the final gating rules and wording before release.

### How food recommendations could work later

The first release plans food the owner already selected. A future verified catalog could offer alternatives only after hard checks: correct species and life stage, complete-food claim, current label data, product availability, and any veterinarian-directed exclusions. Then rank eligible products by owner preferences such as form, pack size, price and ease of purchase. Show why each product appears, what is unknown, and the exact new portion based on its own kcal density. For a reported allergy, do not promise that ingredient filtering proves safety; a veterinarian-directed elimination trial is a separate path. Sponsored placement must not override any safety check. A language model may explain a verified result, but it must not invent a food's adequacy, ingredient list, calories or medical suitability. [AAFCO label guide](https://aafco.org/consumers/understanding-pet-food/reading-labels/), [WSAVA food selection guide](https://wsava.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Selecting-a-pet-food-for-your-pet-updated-2021_WSAVA-Global-Nutrition-Toolkit.pdf), [Merck food allergy review](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/integumentary-system/food-allergy/cutaneous-food-allergy-in-animals).

## Calculation and data contract

For an eligible adult, prefer a reliably measured current intake that is maintaining stable weight and condition. Otherwise use `RER = 70 x weightKg^0.75`, multiplied by a veterinarian-reviewed adult factor appropriate to the known inputs. The factor table and eligibility rules must be versioned. Subtract **known** extras from the starting total, then divide remaining kcal by the exact complete food's `kcal/g = kcal/kg / 1000`. For two complete foods, allocate a calorie share before calculating each food's grams. Split the daily grams among meals and apply rounding only at display or packaging boundaries while keeping the stored total consistent. A can conversion requires that exact can's kcal and net grams. Prefer gram weights to generic cups; a [study of measuring cups](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31409751/) found substantial portion error. [Merck energy equation](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/management-and-nutrition/nutrition-small-animals/nutritional-requirements-of-small-animals), [AAFCO calorie labels](https://aafco.org/consumers/understanding-pet-food/reading-labels/).

**Example for checking software arithmetic only:** 10 kg dog, RER about 394 kcal/day, assumed factor 1.5, 30 kcal known extras, food at 3.5 kcal/g: `(394 x 1.5 - 30) / 3.5` is about 160 g/day, or 80 g in each of two equal meals. This is not an amount to recommend to an individual dog. A 15 kg Shih Tzu entry guard in the existing profile is an implausible-entry check, not an ideal-weight target or energy factor.

Minimum records and important fields:

| ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `PetNutritionSnapshot`                         | pet ID, species, age provenance, life-stage answer, weight plus measured date, 9-point condition plus observer/date, health and restriction answers.                       |
| `FoodProduct`                                  | owner ID, exact name/formula, food role, species/life-stage claim, kcal value and unit, package weight, evidence source/date/photo, verification status.                   |
| `FeedingPlanVersion`                           | pet ID, route, start/end dates, timezone, status, snapshot IDs, daily kcal or manual amount, factor/intake basis, calculation version, warnings, author and approval time. |
| `PlanMeal`                                     | plan version, day rule or exception date, local time, food ID, grams/package amount, kcal snapshot, location and optional caregiver.                                       |
| `FeedingLog`                                   | plan meal ID when available, actual timestamp/amount, fed/partial/skipped/unknown state, extras, observer and notes.                                                       |
| `WeightObservation` and `ConditionObservation` | value, unit or scale, measured date, observer/source and related plan version.                                                                                             |
| Later `InventoryLot` and `Reminder`            | exact product/package/lot and stock; pet/timezone/channel/acknowledgment.                                                                                                  |

The current [pet form](../src/client/pages/planner/pet-form.tsx) splits comma-separated allergies and health conditions into the arrays defined by the [shared schema](../src/shared/pets.ts), but these are unverified text terms. Keep them as distinct reported entries; add reaction status and provenance for planner rules. `currentFood` is only free text, weight has no measurement date, and the current condition field allows 1-10. These need explicit migrations. Keep historical 10-point entries visible as legacy observations without converting them to 9-point scores.

Backend: keep all plan queries scoped to authenticated owner and pet; create separate services for eligibility, unit conversion, calculation, schedule and logging; store immutable plan versions with idempotent Save and log operations. Use a defined local timezone for reminders and calendar days. The week view should read plan versions plus date exceptions, not copy seven near-identical records forever. Edit operations should create a new version for changes to food, daily total or calculation basis. Existing meal logs never change when a later plan changes.

## Suggested screens

1. **Planner home:** pet cards with a clear state (no plan, draft, active, needs review), next meal and recent feeding status. Keep an Add Pet entry but make **Plan meals** the primary action for an existing pet.
2. **Setup:** short steps for health and measurements, food, extras and schedule. Show only relevant questions, save progress, and allow Back without data loss.
3. **Review and confirm:** explicit estimated versus owner/vet-entered amount, daily and per-meal units, food label source, warnings, and Save. No earlier Next button writes an active plan.
4. **Today / week:** planned meals and logged results, quick actions for partial/skipped/extra food, and per-pet switching without mixing records.
5. **Food detail / plan history:** exact food evidence, prior versions, why a change was made, and date it took effect.
6. **Progress:** weight and condition observations alongside average reported intake, with gaps shown as gaps. A trend can prompt review, not diagnosis.

Keep labels short and put help behind an info action. Make units visible beside numbers, keep controls aligned at mobile widths, and test calendar/time input, long product names, keyboard selection, error focus and screen-reader names. Use neutral language such as **starting amount**, **reported feeding**, and **review with your veterinarian**.

## Delivery sequence and release checks

1. **Product and clinical sign-off:** choose launch market and accepted adequacy labels; define the adult factor table, age/weight freshness rule, condition-score guide and high-risk gates with a veterinarian or veterinary nutritionist. Review example outputs and user-facing warnings.
2. **Data foundation:** add dated measurements, 9-point condition observations, structured food and restriction records; migrate without reinterpreting old 10-point values. Version plans and retain calculation inputs.
3. **Core planner:** build setup, two routes, unit-safe calculation, schedule, review and Save. Implement mobile and accessible states before adding reminders.
4. **Daily loop:** add logs, extras, weight review and immutable history. Test multi-pet access and timezones.
5. **Convenience:** reminders, shopping, inventory and product transitions after the basic loop works.

Minimum acceptance cases: dry versus wet food with different kcal units; two foods plus known treats; extras above 10%; missing energy density; stale weight; unknown BCS or neuter status; a puppy near an estimated birthday; a large-breed puppy; an overweight cat; a prescribed renal diet; a reported chicken reaction; two pets eating from each other's bowls; skipped or partial meals; daylight-saving time; food changed after logs exist; and pressing Next on the last setup step without saving. Test the exact calculation and unit conversions, plus the route gates and authorization. Measure setup completion, plans with verified inputs, logs per planned meal, unknown-intake rate, review completion, and reported calculation errors. Do not optimize for the number of plans generated.

Decisions still needed before development: initial country and label standard; dogs and cats together or dogs first; manual label entry versus a curated catalog; who verifies food and condition data; freshness threshold for measured weight; reminder channel; and whether a veterinary partner will review higher-risk cases. These are product choices, not values the energy formula can determine.
