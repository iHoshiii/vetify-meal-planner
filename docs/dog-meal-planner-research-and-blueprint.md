# Vetify Dog Meal Planner: Research and Technical Implementation Blueprint

**Research date:** 9 October 2026, Asia/Singapore. **Scope:** dogs only. **Status:** implementation proposal, not a veterinary-approved formulation library. No application code or database migrations were made for this research task.

The starting source is `Dog_Meal_Planner_Guide.docx`, supplied by the owner. Both pasted task files were identical. All 15 guide sections and 11 tables were read, including the numerical examples. `P` and `T` locators below identify the extracted paragraph and table positions, not page numbers printed in the Word file. The guide has no embedded charts or equations requiring separate image interpretation.

This document distinguishes **guide claims**, **verified external evidence**, and **Vetify design decisions**. A design decision is a proposed product rule, not a newly established veterinary fact. Numerical examples are illustrative estimates for the specified profiles and foods.

## 1. Executive Summary

Build a deterministic dog feeding planner in stages. The first release should calculate portions of an identified, suitable, complete commercial adult dog food for an apparently healthy dog at ideal body condition and stable weight. It should account for every caloric extra, explain the calculation, and record subsequent weight and feeding observations. It should not generate arbitrary homemade meals or therapeutic prescriptions.

Use **FEDIAF September 2025** as the proposed numerical nutritional reference because its current public edition supplies practical canine nutrient recommendations, life-stage distinctions, energy-intake-dependent adult profiles, and documented upper limits. Use **AAHA 2021** for the clinical assessment and initial RER-factor approach. Keep those two rule sets separately versioned. A commercial product's AAFCO adequacy statement remains valid evidence for its stated purpose, rather than being relabeled as FEDIAF-tested. [FEDIAF current edition](https://europeanpetfood.org/self-regulation/nutritional-guidelines/), [AAHA guideline](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/home/), [FDA adequacy guidance](https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/animal-health-literacy/complete-and-balanced-pet-food).

The largest corrections are: no universal senior calorie reduction, no fixed breed or Philippine-temperature multiplier, no unrestricted factor stacking, no generic low-calcium puppy rule, no blanket pregnancy/lactation multiplier, no automatic disease diet, and no completeness claim based on calories, protein, and fat alone. Missing nutrient information must remain unknown.

Vetify already has Expo/React Native, an Express planner API, MongoDB, shared TypeScript calculations, and a separate main account authority. Extend these components after review. The requested relational model is provided as a logical schema with an optional SQL representation, with an explicit MongoDB mapping rather than an assumed database replacement.

## 2. Scope and Assumptions

### 2.1 Supported and deferred behavior

| Capability                                                | First release                            | Later release condition                                                             |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Healthy adult maintenance portions                        | Supported when eligibility checks pass   | Veterinary review of default rules                                                  |
| Owner-entered or veterinarian-prescribed feeding schedule | Recording only, clearly labeled          | No claim that Vetify prescribed or validated it                                     |
| Puppy food portions                                       | No automatic approval initially          | Reviewed growth rules, growth-diet evidence, expected adult-size handling           |
| Weight reduction or gain                                  | Record an existing clinician plan        | Clinician target weight, nutrient-density safeguards, monitoring protocol           |
| Pregnancy, lactation, neonatal or orphan care             | Referral and recording                   | Reproductive specialist approval and validated supervised protocols                 |
| Homemade recipe formulation                               | Disabled                                 | Complete ingredient/premix data, formulation validation, qualified nutrition review |
| Disease-specific diets or elimination trials              | Recording of clinician instructions      | Separate clinical workflow, never an owner-selected disease preset                  |
| Raw recipes                                               | No generation or recommendation          | No assumption that future validation removes infection risk                         |
| AI explanation                                            | Optional, grounded in calculated results | Must never supply missing nutrient values or override blockers                      |

**Product policy:** a blocked automatic plan can still permit a food/weight diary and recording of existing veterinary instructions. It cannot permit an unreviewed calculated recommendation to be saved as approved. Safety restrictions apply equally to Free and Pro accounts.

Existing non-dog records need an explicit migration/read policy; they must not silently become dogs. The proposed dog planner rejects non-dog calculation requests.

### 2.3 Explicit assumptions

All calculation weights are kilograms; energy is kcal of metabolizable energy per day; meals use weighed grams. Food records represent the exact product and preparation state. A breed name does not establish ideal weight, disease, or a diagnosis. Owner health screening is a report of known concerns, not proof of health. Clinical prescriptions and reviews need authenticated, qualified reviewers. Source licensing and Philippine product requirements require verification before production use.

### 5.2 Mandatory screens and stops

For ordinary planning, collect age/growth, measured weight/date, neuter and reproduction answers, BCS, weight stability, activity, known health/appetite concerns, allergy/prescribed-diet status, exact food evidence, extras and meal schedule. Preserve `not_assessed` for MCS. The safe MVP stops automatic approval for growth/uncertain growth, pregnancy/lactation, illness or suspected disease, unexplained weight change, BCS outside 4–5, muscle loss, therapeutic food, allergy trial, raw/homemade primary food, unverified product, missing calories or unknown extras. A qualified assessment can create a separate prescribed recording plan; it cannot mark unknown composition as known. These screening concepts follow [WSAVA nutritional assessment](https://wsava.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/WSAVA-Nutrition-Assessment-Guidelines-2011-JSAP.pdf); the precise MVP gates are Vetify decisions.

### 6.2 Size, meals and transitions

Small, medium, large and giant are useful growth descriptions but are not interchangeable across standards. Store numeric expected adult mass. FEDIAF's late-growth calcium branch uses ** 15 kg**; the AAFCO large-size growth label uses ** 70 lb (about 31.75 kg)**. Store both rules separately. Large and giant dogs may still be growing beyond one year; confirm completion rather than extrapolating an energy equation outside its validated age domain. Excess energy and inappropriate calcium during growth are particular concerns. A complete appropriate growth diet should not receive owner-selected calcium supplements. [AAHA age/breed diets](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/age-specific-and-breed-specific-diets/).

For weaned puppies, three meals through approximately six months and two thereafter are a practical starting schedule; very small puppies and clinical cases may need a different schedule. Adults commonly receive two measured meals. Reproductive feeding frequency is individualized; the lactation example uses four meals only as an illustration. The daily total is determined before distribution. [Merck feeding practices](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/management-and-nutrition/nutrition-small-animals/feeding-practices-in-small-animals).

**Proposed monitoring UI:** offer weekly weight reminders during supervised growth, adult monthly weight recording and earlier review after a food/goal/activity change. Clinicians can change these intervals. Use a gradual 4–7-day food transition for an uncomplicated food change when tolerated, unless the clinician directs otherwise; nutrient eligibility must remain appropriate during the transition. [AAHA healthy feeding plans](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/feeding-plans-for-healthy-appropriate-weight-cats-and-dogs/).

## 7. Energy and Calorie Calculations

### 7.1 RER and one-method selection

Let `W` be the selected body-weight basis in kg:

```text
RER = 70 × W^0.75                  [kcal/day]
Estimated daily energy = RER × f   [kcal/day]
```

The coefficient has implicit dimensions `kcal/day/kg^0.75`. RER estimates resting requirements, not an adequate standalone daily ration for every dog. Use positive measured weight, retain unrounded arithmetic, and never use pounds as kilograms. For 10 kg, `10^0.75 = 5.6234132519`; RER is ** 393.6389276 kcal/day**, approximately 394. [AAHA energy estimates](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/weight-reduction-in-the-obese-pet/).

| AAHA starting context        | Factor `f`  | Weight basis                                     | Vetify use                                                                                          |
| ---------------------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Neutered ordinary adult      | 1.4–1.6     | Current measured weight at appropriate condition | MVP default 1.4 is a proposed initial setting; allow reviewed method version to select within range |
| Intact ordinary adult        | 1.6–1.8     | Current                                          | Proposed default 1.6; assess response                                                               |
| Inactive/obesity-prone adult | 1.0–1.2     | Current if not on a weight-loss plan             | Review, rather than assume this describes every neutered dog                                        |
| Weight reduction             | 1.0         | Clinician-established ideal weight               | Supervised clinical workflow; not generic maintenance-food restriction                              |
| Growing <4 months            | 3.0         | Current                                          | Supervised growth estimate; not neonatal replacement calculation                                    |
| Growing ≥4 months            | 2.0         | Current                                          | Only while growing; large individual variation                                                      |
| Gestation, last 21 days      | 3.0         | Reviewed maternal basis                          | One AAHA estimate; no additional adult/neuter factor                                                |
| Lactation                    | 3.0 to ≥6.0 | Reviewed maternal basis                          | Range only; cannot pick a universal factor from it                                                  |
| Light work                   | 1.6–2.0     | Current at appropriate condition                 | Work context and follow-up required                                                                 |
| Moderate work                | 2.0–5.0     | Current                                          | Specialist review                                                                                   |
| Heavy work                   | 5.0–11.0    | Current                                          | Specialist review; not recreational exercise presets                                                |

Ranges are starting estimates, not bounds on all individuals. Merck gives commonly used adult factors 1.6/1.8 and a different obesity-prone starting estimate; Vetify must identify its chosen method instead of averaging references. [Merck nutritional requirements](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/management-and-nutrition/nutrition-small-animals/nutritional-requirements-of-small-animals).

**Priority:** emergency/health screening → prescribed plan → reproduction → growth → supervised loss/gain → work → ordinary adult maintenance. Select exactly one energy method. For an intact pregnant dog, do not multiply an intact factor by pregnancy factor. For an active neutered adult, select the work method rather than `1.6 × 2`. BCS, age, breed and heat do not add automatic multipliers. If existing measured intake maintains appropriate condition, it is valuable response evidence, but verify extras and weighing before replacing the estimate.

### 7.2 Separate FEDIAF alternatives

FEDIAF 2025 table VII-8 b gives these **alternative**, average estimates. `W` is actual kg body weight; `A` expected mature kg weight; `n` nursing puppies. Store their own method IDs and domains.

```text
Growth, 8 weeks–1 year:
E = [254.1 − 135 × (W/A)] × W^0.75

Gestation, first 4 weeks: E = 132 × W^0.75
Gestation, last 5 weeks: E = 132 × W^0.75 + 26 × W

Lactation, n = 1..4: E = 145 × W^0.75 + 24 × n × W × L
Lactation, n = 5..8: E = 145 × W^0.75 + [96 + 12 × (n−4)] × W × L
L by week 1,2,3,4 = 0.75, 0.95, 1.10, 1.20 respectively
```

All results are kcal/day. The 2025 growth expression and corrected `(n−4)` lactation term must not be replaced by older internet formulas. Unknown expected mature weight, `W>A`, uncertain gestation timing, week outside 1–4 or litter outside 1–8 returns `METHOD_OUT_OF_DOMAIN`; do not clamp or extrapolate. Maternal pregnancy weight can include conceptus mass; the basis needs clinical confirmation. These estimates do not establish a safe recipe or adequate milk production. The newborn reference is `25 × (body weight in grams/100)` kcal/day, but is intentionally not offered as an owner feeding calculator: milk concentration, hydration, age and clinical protocol are required. [FEDIAF 2025, p. 56](https://europeanpetfood.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/FEDIAF-Nutritional-Guidelines_2025-ONLINE.pdf).

### 7.3 Weight goals and follow-up

| Goal/context                       | Basis and method                                                                | Restriction                                                                     |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Healthy maintenance                | Current weight; one adult method; adjust against measured condition/intake      | No unexplained trend or muscle loss                                             |
| Weight reduction                   | Ideal weight confirmed clinically; AAHA 1×RER initial estimate                  | Appropriate weight-management diet, full nutrient density, clinician follow-up  |
| Healthy gain after assessment      | Current condition/intake and clinician's goal                                   | No universal gain factor or automatic rapid increase                            |
| Underweight or unexplained loss    | Clinical evaluation before energy prescription                                  | Diagnose illness/parasites/malabsorption and assess refeeding risk; record only |
| Growth                             | Actual current weight; expected adult weight used only by growth-specific rules | Lean controlled growth, not adult loss/gain algorithm                           |
| Suspected abnormal rapid gain/loss | Confirm measurements then clinical review                                       | Do not compensate silently with a multiplier                                    |

Supervised canine weight loss commonly targets ** 1–2% of current body weight per week**, with reassessment; it is not an automatic demand to lose 2%. AAHA's current diabetic-dog guidance also specifies not exceeding 2% per week and emphasizes clinical context. [AAHA weight reduction](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/weight-reduction-in-the-obese-pet/), [AAHA 2026 diabetes dietary management](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2026-aaha-diabetes-management-guidelines-for-dogs/section-6-dietary-management-in-diabetic-dogs/). Do not derive ideal weight mechanically from a BCS point or breed mean. The response percentage is `(previous kg − current kg)/previous kg ×100`, divided by elapsed weeks when describing a weekly rate.

**Proposed review process:** show trends, flag departure from clinician-approved trajectory, and request re-evaluation. Any routine percentage adjustment step, permitted range and interval must be approved in a protocol before automation; this blueprint does not invent a universal adjustment percentage. Never force intake below a selected standard's validated energy-intake domain without nutrient-density review.

### 8.4 Daily quantities, functions and bioavailability

Convert a profile concentration `q` to a comparison amount `q×E/1000`. For the 629.822284 kcal adult example, A110 protein is ** 28.342003 g/day** and fat ** 8.660056 g/day**. These are profile-derived comparison amounts, not a recommendation to feed only those ingredients. Calculated `E/W^0.75` is 112 kcal/kg^0.75 in that example; the proposed lower intake default `1.4×RER` yields 98 and uses A95 conservatively. Below 95 requires nutrient-density review; do not extrapolate. The exact adult-profile mapping must be reviewer-approved and versioned.

Calcium/phosphorus support skeletal functions; sodium/potassium/chloride electrolyte physiology; magnesium enzymatic functions; iron oxygen transport; copper/iodine/zinc/manganese/selenium distinct enzyme, endocrine and tissue functions. Vitamins A/D/E/K support vision/tissues, calcium regulation, antioxidative functions and coagulation; B vitamins/choline support metabolism and other cellular functions. Deficiency and excess differ by nutrient, chemical form, diet and patient. This is why the validator checks every applicable row rather than a single “vitamin total.” [NRC canine nutrient overview](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/resource/10668/dog_nutrition_final_fix.pdf).

No ingredient's total analytical content proves absorbed availability. Store digestibility, chemical form and retention evidence where known; phytate/fiber, mineral interactions and processing matter. A recipe barely passing numerical minima with uncertain availability cannot receive approval. Do not count an unsuitable mineral form as an adequate available source solely because its elemental total is high. Veterinary formulation review and diet-quality evidence remain required.

## 9. Nutritional Standards and Unit Conversions

### 9.1 Typed arithmetic

Every value carries nutrient ID, unit, denominator, preparation state, evidence type and version. Internally use a canonical mass unit per nutrient (g for macros, mg or µg for appropriate micros), while retaining the source value. Never convert IU without nutrient-specific chemical-form equivalence. Missing moisture prevents a dry-matter conversion; missing ME prevents an energy-basis comparison. A confirmed measured zero is stored as zero; unknown is `null` plus reason.

| Conversion                                         | Formula and independently checked example                                                                                             |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Dry-matter percentage                              | `DM% =100−moisture%`; 10% moisture →90% DM; `DMfraction=0.9`                                                                          |
| AF percent →DM percent, percent-number convention  | `25/0.9=27.777778% DM`; **no additional ×100**                                                                                        |
| AF percent →DM percent, percent/percent convention | `(25/90)×100=27.777778% DM`                                                                                                           |
| Fraction convention                                | `(0.25/0.90)×100=27.777778% DM`                                                                                                       |
| Wet-food comparison                                | 10% AF protein and 75% moisture →`10/0.25=40% DM`; higher DM% does not establish better food                                          |
| Percent →g/100 g                                   | 25% AF =25 g/100 g AF; `g/day=grams_food×25/100`                                                                                      |
| kcal/kg →kcal/g                                    | 3700/1000=3.7 kcal/g                                                                                                                  |
| Package label                                      | 360 kcal per 400 g can →0.9 kcal/g; serving net mass must be known                                                                    |
| g/100 g AF →g/1,000 kcal                           | `25/370×1000=67.567568 g/1000 kcal` for food at 370 kcal/100 g                                                                        |
| mg/100 g →mg/day                                   | `8 mg/100g ×50g/100=4mg/day`                                                                                                          |
| Profile →daily comparison                          | `45 g/1000kcal ×629.822284kcal/1000=28.342003g/day`                                                                                   |
| Food →daily nutrient                               | `170.222239g ×0.25=42.555560g protein/day` if 25% is an exact analytical value; if guaranteed minimum, this is a lower-bound estimate |
| mg↔µg                                              | 1 mg=1000 µg; 0.1 mg selenium=100 µg                                                                                                  |
| kJ↔kcal                                            | 1 kcal=4.184 kJ; 1 MJ≈239.005736 kcal                                                                                                 |
| Ca:P                                               | `total elemental Ca grams/total elemental P grams`; 1.5 g/1 g=1.5:1; P≤0 or unknown cannot yield a passing ratio                      |
| DM legal limit →AF amount                          | `limit_per100gDM ×food_grams×DMfraction/100`; use measured moisture, not assumed food dryness                                         |

These are algebra/unit identities, not new medical claims. Comparison concepts follow [FDA dry-matter/adequacy guidance](https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/animal-health-literacy/complete-and-balanced-pet-food). Keep separate source units even when transformed.

### 9.2 Energy and evidence distinctions

ME is energy available after relevant digestive/urinary losses, distinct from gross energy and human-food label energy. Prefer canine feeding-trial ME or a documented manufacturer's canine ME. If using a modified Atwater estimate, record method and analytical inputs: `ME kcal/100g ≈3.5×protein_g +8.5×fat_g +3.5×NFE_g`, with `NFE%=100−protein−fat−moisture−ash−crude_fiber`. This is an estimate with digestibility limitations, not a safe default when ash is missing or guaranteed minima/maxima are mixed. Do not replace manufacturer ME with that estimate just because labels differ. [AAFCO calorie calculation](https://www.aafco.org/resources/startups/calorie-content/).

### 9.3 Evidence-aware completeness checks

`minimum requirement` is a physiological research concept; `recommended allowance` includes practical availability assumptions; a nutritional profile defines a formulation reference for a stated population; a nutritional maximum and a legal maximum have different purposes. “Complete and balanced” can be supported by an appropriate product's profile/feeding-test declaration. Vetify must distinguish **label-supported suitability** from **computationally assessed recipe**. [FDA complete/balanced evidence](https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/animal-health-literacy/complete-and-balanced-pet-food), [NRC foundational publication](https://www.nationalacademies.org/publications/10668).

The engine propagates intervals: summed lower bounds check minima, summed upper bounds check maxima; a minimum-only guaranteed value cannot certify an upper limit. Missing amino-acid rows cannot be inferred from crude protein. Record `PASS`, `FAIL`, `UNKNOWN` or `NOT_APPLICABLE` per rule. A known failure makes the computed assessment `FAIL`; otherwise any required unknown makes it `INDETERMINATE`; only all applicable checks and qualified review can support a formulated-recipe approval. A label-supported commercial portion plan may proceed without reconstructing every nutrient, but must state that evidence path and cannot claim an independently verified nutrient analysis.

Do not round before comparisons. Preserve decimal precision; round only owner portions, then evaluate the realizable rounded ration and report its small energy difference. Set tolerances solely for floating arithmetic, not to forgive nutrient deficits. Contradictory units, percentage totals above 100, nonfinite values, impossible moisture, mismatched raw/cooked states and contradictory source bounds are data errors, not zero-filled foods.

## 10. Food Database Design

### 10.1 Food records and provenance

Food category is not nutritional evidence. Represent `ingredient`, `commercial_complete`, `commercial_complementary`, `treat`, `supplement` and `reviewed_recipe` separately. A rice ingredient record cannot become a complete food by changing its category without adequacy evidence. Use immutable versions of every product, ingredient preparation and recipe; aliases identify foods but do not replace composition records.

| Record group          | Required fields and validation                                                                                                                    | Use                                                                                               |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Identity              | Stable food ID; version; name; local/English aliases; brand/product/variant; barcode if available; category; dog applicability                    | Exact product and preparation selection; no generic “chicken food” substitution                   |
| Preparation           | Raw/cooked/dehydrated state; method; cut; skin/bone status; edible portion; added ingredients; batch method                                       | Distinguish cooked boneless chicken from raw chicken with bone or seasoned adobo                  |
| Energy                | Value, `kcal/g_AF` canonical unit, original unit/basis; ME method; source; confidence                                                             | Reject ≤0/nonfinite density; unverified human-food energy cannot masquerade as canine measured ME |
| Composition           | Nutrient ID, value or null, unit, denominator, AF/DM/energy basis, exact/min/max/interval status, chemical form                                   | Macros, moisture/ash/fiber type, all applicable AA/FA/mineral/vitamin rows; missing explicit      |
| Yield/retention       | Edible fraction `(0,1]`, raw edible mass, final cooked edible mass, yield factor, nutrient-specific retention source                              | Purchase/raw/cooked conversions; no universal cooking-yield factor                                |
| Serving               | Grams per can/pouch/cup/unit; measuring method; source/date                                                                                       | Package conversion only with net grams; cups secondary to gram scale                              |
| Ingredients/allergens | Canonical ingredients and subingredients; animal taxon; flavor sources; allergen/contamination statements                                         | Safety and prescribed diet-trial checks; `unknown` means unresolved                               |
| Adequacy              | Complete/complementary/unknown; declared standard; formulation/feeding-test evidence; life stage; large-growth statement; exact label text/source | Commercial evidence path, not numerical invention                                                 |
| Safety                | Hazard matches, preparation restrictions, recall/withdrawal status, preservative or supplement limits where supported                             | Known hazard blocks; unresolved compound ingredient prevents automatic approval                   |
| Evidence              | Publisher/record ID/URL, edition/date, retrieved date, license, analytical method/sample count where known, reviewer                              | Traceability; distinguish manufacturer guarantee from laboratory mean                             |
| Currency              | Verified date; review due date; superseded version; approval status; change reason                                                                | No silent mutation of historical calculations                                                     |

A nutrient absence must be explicit: `not_measured`, `not_disclosed`, `not_applicable`, `below_detection_with_limit`, or `unknown`. A detection limit is an interval, not exact zero. The source's analyte matters: total dietary fiber and crude fiber, vitamin activity and mass, carbohydrate by difference and measured starch are distinct.

### 10.2 Composition sources and update workflow

Use [DOST-FNRI PhilFCT](https://i.fnri.dost.gov.ph/fct/library) for locally relevant human-food composition and preparation names, with permitted access and exact record IDs. Use [USDA FoodData Central documentation](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/data-documentation/) to distinguish analytical Foundation records, label-based Branded records and older compilations. Neither establishes canine digestibility or automatically supplies a complete amino-acid/vitamin record. Commercial dog food needs the exact current label and preferably manufacturer nutrient/ME data; verify life-stage evidence and the supplier's quality/nutrition expertise using [WSAVA food-selection questions](https://wsava.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Selecting-a-pet-food-for-your-pet-updated-2021_WSAVA-Global-Nutrition-Toolkit.pdf).

No individual PhilFCT or USDA ingredient values are invented in this blueprint. The initial catalog can list candidate identities, but a production composition record is unavailable until its permitted source, cooking state and fields are reviewed. Avoid copying one food's missing values from another variety or using generic fish numbers for bangus.

**Proposed update process:** import into quarantine → validate units/ranges/preparation → compare prior version → check safety/recalls/adequacy → independent reviewer approves → publish immutable version → flag affected active plans → require recalculation/review where material. A monthly recall/formulation check and at least annual catalog review are product service targets, not claims that labels remain valid for a year. Urgent safety notices invalidate eligibility immediately. Retain source hash and audit actor; rollback selects an older approved version but cannot erase a withdrawal.

Local identity and composition sourcing: [PhilFCT](https://i.fnri.dost.gov.ph/login/fct), [USDA record types](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/data-documentation/). A plain ingredient is different from its seasoned dish. Ingredient filters must support `sibuyas`, `bawang`, cocoa/cacao, raisins/pasas and known xylitol aliases, while unknown dishes remain unresolved rather than being guessed safe.

### 11.3 Handling in a hot, humid setting

PAGASA describes broad dry **December–May** and rainy **June–November** seasons, with regional/altitude differences. Local measured heat affects storage decisions; the app must not automatically lower calories 5–10% because the location is the Philippines. [PAGASA climate](https://www.pagasa.dost.gov.ph/information/climate-philippines).

Use clean utensils and separate raw-food surfaces. Cook poultry to ** 74°C**, fish to ** 63°C**, ground meat to ** 71°C**, and egg dishes to ** 71°C**; eggs' whites/yolks should be firm. Reheat suitable cooked leftovers to ** 74°C** using a food thermometer. These food-safety temperatures do not establish nutritional completeness or remove chemical toxins/bones. [Official temperature chart, reviewed 21 November 2024](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures).

Perishable prepared food must not remain unrefrigerated beyond ** 2 hours**, or ** 1 hour above 32.2°C (90°F)**; use a stricter product instruction where present. Refrigerate promptly in shallow covered containers; refrigerator target **≤4°C** is a conservative operational choice within the official ≤40°F limit. Properly handled general cooked leftovers have a ** 3–4-day** refrigerated window, subject to shorter ingredient/product directions; an unknown time/temperature history means discard. This is not a guarantee for every recipe or contaminated batch. [USDA leftovers](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety), [USDA two-hour rule](https://ask.fsis.usda.gov/article/What-is-the-2-Hour-Rule-with-leaving-food-out).

Retain dry food in its original closed bag for lot/expiry identification, inside a clean suitable container if needed; keep cool/dry and follow its label. Promptly cover/refrigerate unused canned food, wash bowls/utensils and discard spoiled food. A sealed container cannot make hot damp food safe. [FDA handling/storage](https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/animal-health-literacy/tips-safe-handling-pet-food-and-treats). Proposed UI fields: prepared/opened time, stored temperature, use-by instruction, batch ID and discard flag. They are reminders, not microbial testing. Heat-related collapse or marked appetite change needs veterinary attention, rather than calorie reduction to conceal it.

Commercial claims depend on the declared standard, purpose and life stage; feeding-test evidence and formulation-profile evidence are different routes. “All life stages” is not a senior-specific therapeutic prescription. Marketing terms, an ingredient list, cost or the presence/absence of grain cannot replace adequacy/manufacturer evidence. [FDA nutritional adequacy](https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/animal-health-literacy/complete-and-balanced-pet-food), [WSAVA selection guidance](https://wsava.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Selecting-a-pet-food-for-your-pet-updated-2021_WSAVA-Global-Nutrition-Toolkit.pdf).

Home-prepared variety does not ensure adequate calcium, trace nutrients, AA or FA. Adding a generic multivitamin or eggshell does not establish balance and may create excess. UC Davis evaluated 200 recipes from 34 sources and found substantial nutritional problems; its research supports qualified formulation, not a universal recipe. [UC Davis 2013 study report](https://www.ucdavis.edu/news/homemade-dog-food-recipes-can-be-risky-business-study-finds). Require a validated exact premix/dose; omitted oil, liver, mineral or replacement meat changes a reviewed recipe. A plan that is calorie-sufficient but calcium-deficient is a failure, even if visually varied.

**Commercial validation path:** label evidence suitable for this dog →safety/health screens →ME and ledger →portions →label-supported status. **Formulated-recipe path:** complete typed data →actual-ration nutrient totals/densities →all minima/ratios/maxima and forms →availability/processing review →signed formulation approval. Unknown required rows return `INDETERMINATE`, not “balanced.” A 90%-complete-food rule for extras is a practical feeding guardrail, not proof every resulting nutrient concentration passes the full mathematical profile.

## 13. Portion and Meal Distribution

### 13.1 Calorie ledger

```text
E_target = selected daily energy
E_extras = sum(treats + toppers + caloric supplements + medication foods)
E_primary = E_target − E_extras
food_i_kcal = E_primary × allocation_i
grams_i = food_i_kcal / exact_food_i_kcal_per_gram
sum(allocation_i) = 1; all allocations ≥0
```

Each intake item has a unique ledger ID and exactly one role. A fish-oil supplement contributes energy once and nutrients once; a topper cannot also appear as a second treat line. Explicitly validate `0≤E_extras<E_target`, identified energy, suitable foods and a workable primary ration. For an ordinary label-supported plan, extras should be **≤10% of target energy**, with primary complete food ≥90%. Clinical plans can require stricter rules. [AAHA healthy feeding plans](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/feeding-plans-for-healthy-appropriate-weight-cats-and-dogs/).

### 13.3 Rounding, meals and kitchen yield

**Proposed deterministic display policy:** round each daily food grams to 0.1 g using decimal half-up; distribute the rounded total by recorded meal shares. For equal meals, floor each of the first `n−1` shares to 0.1 g and assign the remaining quantity to the final meal. This guarantees nonnegative meals summing to the displayed daily amount. Recalculate actual displayed ration energy; very small doses requiring finer scales or an impractical final remainder need a finer approved resolution/review, not a forced zero-dose instruction. The examples use this policy, hence 148.9 g splits 74.4/74.5 g, and 467.8 g splits 116.9/116.9/116.9/117.1 g. Unequal meal shares must sum to 1 and obey any prescribed schedule.

A display rounding tolerance is a product usability rule and must be published/reviewed; propose ≤1% target-energy deviation for ordinary portions, otherwise flag `PORTION_RESOLUTION_UNSUITABLE`. This tolerance cannot make nutrient failures pass. Compare nutrient **concentrations in the actual ration** and ratio constraints using unrounded computed values from its displayed quantities; show target-derived daily comparison amounts separately. Clinical prescriptions may need different precision.

Yield example: purchase 500 g at edible fraction 0.8 →400 g edible raw; measured cooked edible mass 300 g →yield 0.75. To obtain 150 g cooked requires 200 g edible raw, or 250 g purchased raw under that specific yield. Use the composition record for **cooked food** at 150 g. Alternatively compute nutrient-retained batch totals from raw composition and nutrient-specific validated retention factors, then divide by measured final batch mass. Do not apply both methods; do not assume all nutrients retain 100% or that moisture loss creates nutrients. Batch gravy/oil retained or discarded needs explicit handling. Unvalidated yields/retentions prevent formulation approval.

Substitution must rerun ingredient/allergen/clinical eligibility, composition coverage, energy, full nutrient constraints and review status. Equal grams and equal calories are insufficient. A reviewed recipe becomes unreviewed when an ingredient, premix, dose or cooking method changes materially.

### 15.1 Elimination trials and hidden exposure

Food allergy involves immune response; intolerance does not imply the same immune mechanism. Signs overlap and cannot be diagnosed from a checklist. A veterinarian-managed elimination trial uses an appropriate prescription hydrolyzed or genuinely suitable novel-protein diet, strict control of **all** intake, assessment and typically provocation to support diagnosis. AAHA describes trials lasting 4–12 weeks, with eight weeks capturing most food-allergic responses and some requiring twelve. OTC “limited ingredient” foods may have unlisted protein contamination. [AAHA 2023 allergy guideline](https://www.aaha.org/wp-content/uploads/globalassets/02-guidelines/2023-aaha-management-of-allergic-skin-diseases-in-dogs-and-cats-guidelines/resources/2023-aaha-management-of-allergic-skin-diseases-guidelines.pdf).

Vetify may record the prescribed trial, food/treat/chew/flavored medication exposure, symptoms and dates. It must not recommend stopping medication, label a purified fat as allergenic solely by species name, assume a fat has no residual protein, or choose a trial protein only because the owner has not fed it knowingly. Require ingredient/formulation and contamination evidence matched to the prescription. Preferences and suspected reactions remain separately labeled; symptom improvement alone does not prove the cause.

### 15.2 Escalation

Potential toxin ingestion, collapse, breathing difficulty, seizures, severe abdominal distension/unproductive retching, inability to swallow or marked dehydration require urgent veterinary guidance. Persistent vomiting/diarrhea, appetite loss or abnormal weight/muscle change requires assessment before a new calculated plan. Owners cannot dismiss a structured safety blocker to obtain automatic approval. Veterinary authorization controls a scoped clinical workflow and never bypasses a known toxin or turns unknown nutrient data into passing measurements.

If a raw diet is already fed, the diary can record it with appropriate risk information, without a recommendation flag.

### 16.3 Nutrient mathematics and future recipe solver

For food `i` at quantity `x_i` grams and canonical nutrient concentration `a_ij` per gram, `N_j=Σ(x_i×a_ij)`. Unknown contributing concentration produces an unknown interval unless a defensible bound exists. `E_ration=Σ(x_i×ME_i)`. For profile minimum `m_j`, check `N_j≥m_j×E_ration/1000`; maximum `u_j` checks `N_j≤u_j×E_ration/1000`. A separate target-derived daily comparison is educational and must be identified as such.

Ratios use summed elemental mass: `Ca≥r_min×P` and `Ca≤r_max×P`, with known positive P. A DM limit `d_j` per 100 g DM requires `N_j≤d_j×Σ(x_i×DMfraction_i)/100`. Organic selenium added amount is a separate contributor total, not inferred from total selenium. For wet/dry selenium profile distinctions in an actual mixed formulation, require a documented reviewer-approved mapping; do not choose the lower value for convenience.

A future solver can use nonnegative ingredient quantities, typed coefficients, approved ingredient bounds, energy bounds, all nutrient inequalities and ratio constraints. Generate candidates only from reviewed exact ingredients/premixes/preparations. Specify reproducible optimization order: feasible safety/adequacy first; then minimize target-energy deviation; then deviation from approved recipe/preferences; then stable food-ID tie-break. Quantity limits and recipe patterns must have sources/reviewer approval, not invented universal maxima. Solver tolerances must not relax nutrition. `INFEASIBLE` yields the conflicting constraints and no approved recipe; `DATA_INCOMPLETE` does not run a solver with zeros. Solving the matrix still does not replace availability, cooking and veterinary formulation review.

Monitoring stores weight/BCS/MCS, offered/eaten food, extras and symptoms with timestamps. A new observation proposes reassessment without editing the historical plan. A source update, food withdrawal, neutering, suspected illness, pregnancy, changed activity or significant unexplained trend invalidates reliance on an old automatic recommendation until the relevant workflow runs again. AI may explain the recorded results using source excerpts approved for use, but deterministic modules own quantities, eligibility and approval.

## 17. Database Schema

### 17.1 Relational specification and repository mapping

This is a **logical relational design**, not a proposal to replace the existing MongoDB immediately. SQL types make constraints explicit; map them to TypeScript/MongoDB validators, indexed references and transactions during implementation. Current main-user IDs are externally issued strings (currently 24-hex ObjectIds). They are not planner passwords/accounts. Use `owner_id varchar(128)` plus trusted issuer; do not create a local users table or an unenforceable cross-database foreign key.

Common conventions: IDs `uuid` in a possible SQL implementation, current MongoDB `ObjectId` in the retained system; `numeric(18,8)` for typed quantities; `timestamptz` in UTC for events; `date` for birth/calendar dates; positive `integer` revisions; explicit enums/checks; JSONB only for immutable full snapshots or typed nested data. `created_at` and `updated_at` are required on mutable records. Absence uses SQL NULL/Mongo null plus a reason. A source string is not a numeric value. SQL/Mongo identifiers may differ through an explicit mapping, never by truncation.

### 17.3 Illustrative SQL constraints and MongoDB implementation

The SQL is illustrative documentation, not an executed migration. A future SQL version can enforce:

```sql
CHECK (weight_kg IS NULL OR weight_kg > 0)
CHECK (bcs IS NULL OR bcs BETWEEN 1 AND 9)
CHECK (lower_value IS NULL OR upper_value IS NULL OR lower_value <= upper_value)
UNIQUE (dog_id, version)
UNIQUE (owner_id, request_id)
-- Use a partial unique index on dog_id for active published plans.
-- Validate interval overlap, same-owner references and allocation sums in a transaction.
```

For current MongoDB, extend existing `pets` with the dog profile or use a dog-specific profile collection linked to the existing ID. Keep historical `meal_plans`, `feeding_logs` and `nutrition_observations`; add standard/food/source/calculation/review collections with explicit validators. Embed small immutable plan snapshots and meal allocations; reference large food catalogs and versions. Map SQL unique constraints to compound/partial indexes and optimistic version checks. `Decimal128` or a documented precision strategy preserves decimal quantities; JS numeric results need canonical serialization and bounded comparison tolerance.

Publishing a replacement plan must atomically end the old active plan and create the new version, with owner/request idempotency. MongoDB multi-document transactions require a suitable replica-set deployment; the current local standalone memory-server setup does not prove transaction support. Choose a tested replica-set development configuration or a single-document active-pointer/version design with compare-and-swap and recovery, before relying on transactions. Existing non-dog records remain readable only through an explicit compatibility policy, outside the dog calculation feature. Account deletion/retention must follow actual Vetify account events and configured data-retention policy; do not build a second authentication authority as part of nutrition work.

## 20. Testing and Validation

### 20.1 Independent oracles and fixture rules

Use independently computed constants, source-table double-entry and reviewer-approved fixture diets. The research calculations used Python Decimal at 50-digit precision with repeated square roots; they did not call the repository's calculation engine. Unit-test expected values should be stored constants plus a documented derivation, not generated by the implementation under test. Formula tolerances: propose absolute `1e-6` kcal/g for golden numerical comparisons where output precision permits; use exact decimal rules for displayed amounts and schema checks. Verify a different implementation/manual calculation for release.

Below, numerical adequacy fixtures are **synthetic test data**, not edible recipes or approved diets. Unless specified, all other applicable nutrient rows are exact and safely satisfy the selected reference; thus one altered row isolates a meaningful failure. This prevents tests from accidentally failing for an unrelated missing nutrient. The method and evidence path are explicit.

### 20.4 Release validation

Perform unit tests for pure engines, integration tests against an isolated database/auth fixture, contract tests for main principal verification, mobile acceptance tests for forms/errors/units and nutritional review with independent reference calculations. Property checks: nonnegative finite amounts; meal sums equal daily totals; energy ledger sums equal actual ration; substitution always changes/rechecks affected evidence; missing values remain missing through serialization; historical replay uses identical inputs/versions. Use cases at exact age, size, calcium, ratio, extra-budget and precision boundaries.

A laboratory/feeding evidence review is separate from software tests: passing arithmetic does not prove palatability, digestibility, manufacturing quality or long-term health. Never use the seven examples as clinical outcome evidence. Track known limitations, reviewed scope and source/engine versions in the release report. All clinical/formulated population gates remain disabled until separately reviewed.

## 21. Limitations and Veterinary Oversight

This blueprint does not certify Vetify diets, diagnose dogs or establish a validated recipe library. Energy equations estimate an initial intake. Individual response, measurement quality, ingredient variability, digestibility and clinical state matter. Profile tables address stated healthy populations; legal limits have jurisdictions and chemical-form conditions. Commercial adequacy declarations and computational checks provide different evidence.

The unresolved release decisions are explicit:

| Decision                                       | Required resolution before enabling the affected feature                                                                     |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Numeric standard package                       | Veterinary double-entry verification, FEDIAF source-rights permission and current edition check                              |
| AAFCO exact compliance                         | Access and review of licensed 2026 canine profiles/footnotes; do not claim verified 2026 limits from a 2014 public file      |
| Philippine regulatory obligations              | Qualified local verification for product labeling, claims and applicable limits; this is not a legal-compliance assessment   |
| Adult 95/110 mapping and low-energy diets      | Reviewer approves conservative mapping and density domain; clinical review below 95                                          |
| Growth/calcium timing and uncertain adult mass | Reviewed transition evidence, age/size confidence and Great Dane exception; no forced birthday switch                        |
| Reproductive models                            | Reviewed weight basis, litter/week domain, monitoring and exact reproduction nutrient limits                                 |
| Ingredient nutrition/ME/yield                  | Permitted exact data, source matching, canine energy method, retention and availability review; malunggay remains unapproved |
| Homemade completeness                          | Complete food/premix data, formulation review and reproducible preparation; solver success alone insufficient                |
| Clinical prescription/reviewer identity        | Verified qualification, scope, dates and authenticated review workflow                                                       |
| Operational database publication               | Tested Mongo transaction/CAS strategy, idempotency, historical replay and recovery                                           |
| Adjustment automation                          | Clinician-approved thresholds/intervals and monitoring; no universal 10%change rule                                          |

Until resolved, expose recording and educational explanation while blocking unvalidated recommendations. Persistent signs, weight/muscle loss, uncertain pregnancy, nutritional deficiencies or suspected exposure need appropriate veterinary assessment. Referral text must be usable locally; do not invent a Philippine poison-hotline number or imply a US service is locally available without verification.
