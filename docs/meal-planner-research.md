# Pet meal planner: evidence and proposed requirements

Research checked 30 September 2026. This is a product planning document, not a feeding prescription. It covers dogs and cats only. Recommendations below distinguish published guidance from Vetify's proposed product rules.

## Decision

The first useful version should schedule and measure an existing **complete, balanced food** for an apparently healthy adult dog or cat. Its portion calculator should show an **initial estimate**, the inputs and assumptions behind it, and a prompt to review weight and body condition. A schedule can also record an amount prescribed by a veterinarian without changing that amount. It should not invent recipes, diagnose allergies, choose a therapeutic diet, or describe a calculated portion as exact. This is a product-safety inference from the evidence below, not a claim that every animal outside this scope cannot be fed safely.

The existing [PRD](PRD.md) says age, weight, and allergies can produce a personalized seven-day plan and describes single-pet support. Both statements should be revised before building: a seven-day calendar does not establish nutritional adequacy, and Vetify now supports multiple pets per account.

## Evidence that changes the design

1. **Energy equations are estimates.** Resting energy requirement (RER) is `70 × current body weight in kg^0.75` kcal/day. Maintenance energy requirement (MER) applies a life-stage/activity factor, but factors vary by animal and publication. An individual can need roughly 30% more or less than the calculated value. FEDIAF explicitly warns that no one equation fits all pets and recommends adjusting against body weight and condition. The formula cannot identify an ideal weight from breed or current weight alone. [Merck Veterinary Manual](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/management-and-nutrition/nutrition-small-animals/nutritional-requirements-of-small-animals), [AAHA energy calculations](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/weight-reduction-in-the-obese-pet/), [FEDIAF 2025 guidelines](https://europeanpetfood.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/FEDIAF-Nutritional-Guidelines_2025-ONLINE.pdf).
2. **Calories alone do not make a diet complete.** Dogs and cats need suitable concentrations of amino acids, fats, vitamins, and minerals, and cats require dietary taurine, preformed vitamin A, and arachidonic acid. Calcium/phosphorus balance matters especially during growth. Use a food's species and life-stage adequacy claim rather than trying to infer adequacy from its ingredient list or from calories. AAFCO and FEDIAF are frameworks for assessing complete foods; they are not endorsements of individual brands. [Merck nutritional requirements](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/management-and-nutrition/nutrition-small-animals/nutritional-requirements-of-small-animals), [AAFCO reading labels](https://aafco.org/consumers/understanding-pet-food/reading-labels/), [FEDIAF 2025](https://europeanpetfood.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/FEDIAF-Nutritional-Guidelines_2025-ONLINE.pdf), [WSAVA selecting a pet food](https://wsava.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Selecting-a-pet-food-for-your-pet-updated-2021_WSAVA-Global-Nutrition-Toolkit.pdf).
3. **Actual feeding history matters.** A nutritional assessment includes weight, body condition score (BCS), muscle condition, life stage, health, main food, treats, table food, chews, supplements, food used for medication, feeding access, and recent changes. AAHA treats BCS outside 4–5/9, muscle loss, unexplained weight change, disease, and growth/pregnancy/lactation as reasons for deeper assessment. An owner-entered BCS is useful but should not be treated as clinician-confirmed. [AAHA nutritional risk factors](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/nutritional-risk-factors/), [AAHA feeding plans](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/feeding-plans-for-healthy-appropriate-weight-cats-and-dogs/), [WSAVA nutrition toolkit](https://wsava.org/global-guidelines/global-nutrition-guidelines/).
4. **Food labels must be used carefully.** AAFCO labels distinguish complete food for a specified species and life stage from treats or supplemental foods and report calorie density in kcal/kg plus a familiar unit on US labels. Calorie information is not required on all non-US labels, according to WSAVA. Vetify should request the exact product and package label or manufacturer figure; it cannot use a generic breed or food-type calorie value. [AAFCO reading labels](https://aafco.org/consumers/understanding-pet-food/reading-labels/), [WSAVA selecting a pet food](https://wsava.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Selecting-a-pet-food-for-your-pet-updated-2021_WSAVA-Global-Nutrition-Toolkit.pdf).
5. **Home recipes are a separate clinical problem.** AAHA recommends consultation with a board-certified veterinary nutritionist for home-prepared diets. A UC Davis analysis of 200 published dog-food recipes found 95% deficient in at least one essential nutrient. Rotating such recipes does not reliably repair the deficits. Raw animal foods add pathogen risk for pets and people. A meal planner must not present generated ingredient combinations as complete diets. [AAHA home-prepared diets](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/home-prepared-diets/), [UC Davis original study summary](https://ccah.vetmed.ucdavis.edu/sites/g/files/dgvnsk4586/files/local_resources/pdfs/Homemade_dog_food_recipes_July-15-2013_UCD-News.pdf), [FDA raw-food study](https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/animal-health-literacy/get-facts-raw-pet-food-diets-can-be-dangerous-you-and-your-pet).

## A. Pet profile: keep the current form, add provenance

The account-to-pets relationship in the README and implementation is correct. Name is required to identify a pet. Keep species, age, and measured current weight required as the owner requested. Keep optional breed, birth **month/year**, food preferences, and health notes. Sex can remain `unknown`; spay/neuter can remain `prefer_not_to_say`. Neither breed nor sex gives a reliable substitute for a measured weight or missing neuter status. Activity should be a simple owner description, not a multiplier selected without explanation.

For the planner, collect the following **when making a plan**, instead of making the Add Pet form longer:

| Item                                                                                  | Need for portion estimate                                       | Why                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Measured weight **and date measured**                                                 | Required                                                        | Old weight can make a new calculation misleading. Never infer it from breed.                                                         |
| BCS using a dog/cat 9-point picture guide; record `owner` or `veterinarian` as source | Required for automatic eligibility assessment; can be `unknown` | Weight alone does not say whether the pet is lean, ideal, or overweight. Unknown BCS means schedule-only or seek a veterinary check. |
| Weight trend and current intake                                                       | Strongly recommended                                            | A stable pet already eating a measured amount provides a better individual starting point than a population equation alone.          |
| Health/medication screen, pregnancy/nursing, growing status, current veterinary diet  | Required yes/no/unknown screen                                  | Determines whether automatic calculation is appropriate. Unknown is not the same as no.                                              |
| Exact food, adequacy statement, kcal per kg or kcal per package, and source/date      | Required to calculate grams                                     | The present `currentFood` free text cannot supply calories or nutritional adequacy.                                                  |
| Every other daily calorie source                                                      | Required, or marked unknown                                     | Treats, chews, toppers, and food given with medicines count too.                                                                     |
| Feeding pattern and other pets' access to the food                                    | Required for an actionable schedule                             | In multi-pet homes, food assigned to one animal may be eaten by another.                                                             |

Birth month/year is still optional. If entered, Vetify's last-day-of-month birthday rule is a **display convention**, because the actual day is unknown. If birth month is absent, the registration-anniversary rule advances an **age estimate**, not a verified birthday. Keep `age source`, `estimated versus known`, and `last confirmed on`; display “about 1 year” when appropriate. Do not automatically switch growth to adult food because that estimated age crossed a calendar threshold. For a growing pet, age and expected adult size need veterinarian/owner confirmation. [AAHA age and breed diets](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/age-specific-and-breed-specific-diets/).

## B–C. What the planner can calculate

For an eligible, apparently healthy adult dog or cat in appropriate body condition:

1. Prefer a measured **current intake that is maintaining stable weight and condition** as the initial daily energy budget, if it can be reconstructed reliably. Otherwise estimate RER and apply a documented adult MER factor from AAHA/WSAVA. If spay/neuter is private or activity is unknown, do not silently choose a factor; ask for current intake or offer schedule-only. The factor is an initial assumption, not a diagnosis. [AAHA feeding plans](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/feeding-plans-for-healthy-appropriate-weight-cats-and-dogs/), [AAHA MER factors](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/weight-reduction-in-the-obese-pet/).
2. Count calories from all food sources. AAHA's general rule is that at least 90% of daily calories should come from complete, balanced food and extras should be at most 10%. **Do not subtract 10% automatically** if no treats are planned. If extras exceed the limit, flag the diet for review; simply reducing complete food further can worsen nutrient balance. [AAHA feeding plans](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/feeding-plans-for-healthy-appropriate-weight-cats-and-dogs/), [AAHA nutritional risk factors](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/nutritional-risk-factors/).
3. Compute `complete-food kcal/day = initial daily kcal − known calories from extras`. With `kcal/g = label kcal/kg ÷ 1000`, compute `food grams/day = complete-food kcal/day ÷ kcal/g`. If feeding two foods, assign a calorie share to each first and calculate each separately. Split the resulting daily grams into chosen meals. For cans or pouches, convert using the **exact package** kcal and net weight. Do not use a generic cup conversion. A study found household dry-food measuring cups can be substantially inaccurate; a gram scale is preferable. [AAFCO calorie labeling](https://aafco.org/consumers/understanding-pet-food/reading-labels/), [measuring-cup study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31409751/).
4. Show the calculation as an **initial feeding amount**, along with the food label source, the pet weight date, and a request to monitor intake, weight, BCS, and appetite. Recalculate after a meaningful change in food, weight, activity, health, or treats. There is no honest fixed number of grams that will be right for all pets of the same weight. [FEDIAF 2025](https://europeanpetfood.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/FEDIAF-Nutritional-Guidelines_2025-ONLINE.pdf), [AAHA feeding plans](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/feeding-plans-for-healthy-appropriate-weight-cats-and-dogs/).

Illustration only: a **10 kg** dog has RER about **394 kcal/day**. An assumed adult factor of **1.5** gives about **590 kcal/day**. If known treats supply **30 kcal** and the exact complete food supplies **3.5 kcal/g**, the food amount starts at about **160 g/day**. This is arithmetic, **not** a recommendation for a real 10 kg dog; the factor and suitable total must be checked against that dog's condition and subsequent weight. [AAHA MER factors](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/weight-reduction-in-the-obese-pet/), [Merck energy uncertainty](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/management-and-nutrition/nutrition-small-animals/nutritional-requirements-of-small-animals).

Reported food allergies are not universal species rules. The commonly reported sources are beef, dairy, chicken, wheat, and lamb in dogs, and beef, fish, and chicken in cats, largely reflecting what animals have been fed. A pet may react to multiple sources, and an intolerance or toxic reaction is different from a confirmed allergy. Do not preemptively ban these common foods from every pet. [Merck food allergy review](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/integumentary-system/food-allergy/cutaneous-food-allergy-in-animals). Likewise, published grape/raisin kidney-injury cases are primarily in dogs, with insufficient cat evidence to call grapes safe for cats. Xylitol's severe hypoglycemia/liver risk is documented in dogs; the same degree of cat toxicity is not established. Vetify should avoid proposing either as a pet snack without falsely claiming identical species-specific evidence. [Merck grape/raisin review](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/toxicology/food-hazards/grape-raisin-and-tamarind-vitis-spp-tamarindus-spp-toxicosis-in-dogs), [ASPCA food hazards](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/people-foods-avoid-feeding-your-pets).

Fresh water should remain available; wet food changes how much a pet drinks, so do not output a universal water quantity as if it were a prescription. [Merck water guidance](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/management-and-nutrition/nutrition-small-animals/nutritional-requirements-of-small-animals).

## E. Frequency, variety, and seven-day flow

Do not encode one mandatory meal count for every dog or cat. Merck suggests measured adult-dog meals, commonly twice daily; young puppies generally need more frequent meals, and growth feeding changes with age. VCA notes that adult cats often do well with at least two meals and some do better with three or four; kittens can benefit from smaller, more frequent meals. A medical condition can change timing. Meal count is a **schedule setting**, with life-stage guidance, rather than the input that determines total calories. [Merck feeding practices](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/management-and-nutrition/nutrition-small-animals/feeding-practices-in-small-animals), [VCA cat feeding frequency](https://vcahospitals.com/boulevard/know-your-pet/feeding-times-and-frequency-for-cats).

A complete, balanced diet does not need a new recipe every day to become nutritious. Weekly variety is optional and complicates calorie and allergy checks. If changing products, AAHA suggests transitioning over **4–7 days** to reduce digestive upset; that is guidance, not a guarantee. [AAHA feeding plans](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/feeding-plans-for-healthy-appropriate-weight-cats-and-dogs/), [UC Davis recipe study](https://ccah.vetmed.ucdavis.edu/sites/g/files/dgvnsk4586/files/local_resources/pdfs/Homemade_dog_food_recipes_July-15-2013_UCD-News.pdf).

Suggested flow: **choose pet → check eligibility and data freshness → choose verified food or existing vet prescription → enter extras and feeding times → calculate a starting amount if eligible → show the source and assumptions → create a repeatable seven-day schedule → log actual intake/weight/BCS and reassess.** The seven-day view is a calendar, not evidence that a diet has been nutritionally validated.

## F–H. Release boundaries

| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Healthy, weight-stable adult dog/cat; current measured weight, BCS, suitable complete food, known food calories and extras                                                                        | Generate schedule plus clearly labeled **starting** portion estimate.                                                         | Closest fit to the published adult-maintenance guidance.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Missing label calories, stale/unmeasured weight, unknown BCS, private neuter status with no reliable intake history, uncertain age near a growth boundary                                         | Schedule only from an owner/vet-entered amount; request missing information before automatic portion estimate.                | A precise gram amount would depend on invented inputs. This is Vetify's conservative product rule.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| Growing puppy/kitten, large-breed puppy, pregnant/nursing animal, known disease, under/overweight pet, unexplained weight or appetite change, serious food reaction, veterinary prescription diet | Allow an existing veterinarian-provided food/amount to be scheduled; do not calculate a new therapeutic or growth plan in v1. | These are nutritional risk factors requiring individualized assessment. [AAHA nutritional risk factors](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/nutritional-risk-factors/), [AAHA age and breed diets](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/age-specific-and-breed-specific-diets/). |
| Species other than dog/cat; homemade or raw recipe request; unknown food adequacy                                                                                                                 | Do not generate a nutritionally endorsed plan.                                                                                | Evidence/model scope and product data are insufficient.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |

The MVP should include one account with many pets, pet-specific food records, an eligibility questionnaire, food-label entry with source/date, transparent portion math for eligible adults, repeatable meal times, extras tracking, and a weight/BCS follow-up. Avoid a broad product catalog until its provenance and update process are defined. Food preferences can affect the owner's choice among otherwise suitable foods; they cannot override a medical restriction or adequacy check. No protein/fat/carbohydrate percentage targets are needed in the MVP because a verified complete food handles the nutrient balance and most labels do not expose enough analysis to validate custom nutrient totals.

Later work could add growth support with expected adult size and growth charts, clinician-reviewed weight management, dietitian-formulated home recipes, product integrations with label review, and condition-specific workflows developed with veterinarians. Each needs its own evidence and testing. “Senior” alone should not trigger a special diet or automatic calorie reduction: AAFCO has no separate senior adequacy stage, and older cats may need **more** energy while older dogs may need less or vary individually. [AAHA age and breed diets](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/age-specific-and-breed-specific-diets/), [AAHA obesity prevention](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/prevention-of-obesity/).

For a future meal-planner microservice, keep pet identity separate from nutrition inputs. A saved plan should reference `ownerId` and `petId` but also retain the pet weight/date, age provenance, food label/calories/source, extras, chosen factor or observed intake, calculation version, timezone, and creation date used at generation. This prevents old plans from silently changing when a profile or product is edited. This is an engineering recommendation; it is not a veterinary claim.

Before release, have a veterinarian or board-certified veterinary nutritionist review the **eligibility rules, wording, formula factors, and example outputs**. Test at least these cases: the same pet with dry versus wet food and different kcal units; two complete foods plus treats; unknown neuter status; an estimated puppy age near one year; a large-breed puppy; an overweight cat; a prescribed renal diet; a reported chicken reaction with a chicken-derived ingredient; a product with no verified kcal figure; and two pets with access to each other's food. These are acceptance cases proposed for Vetify, not clinical studies.

## Source hierarchy and remaining uncertainty

Use [FEDIAF's current 2025 guideline](https://www.fediaf.org/self-regulation/nutritional-guidelines/) and [AAFCO label guidance](https://aafco.org/consumers/understanding-pet-food/reading-labels/) for food adequacy; [NRC's nutrient reference](https://www.nationalacademies.org/publications/10668) for foundational nutrient science; [AAHA](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/home/) and [WSAVA](https://wsava.org/global-guidelines/global-nutrition-guidelines/) for clinical assessment and feeding practice; [Merck](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/management-and-nutrition/nutrition-small-animals/nutritional-requirements-of-small-animals) for peer-reviewed clinical synthesis; and primary studies for specific measurements or allergy-label limitations. These standards come from different markets. Do not assume every locally sold package has a US AAFCO statement or printed calorie content.

Unresolved before implementation: target launch market and accepted label formats; whether Vetify will support manual food entry only or a curated verified catalog; who can verify BCS and food-label data; a stale-weight threshold and follow-up interval suitable for the app; and which, if any, higher-risk cases a veterinarian partner will review. Those are product decisions or require clinical input, not facts that a calorie formula can settle.
